02 · How it's built

The rules that still hold

  • One design, Lens, on both. iOS draws it in Liquid Glass (@expo/ui/swift-ui); Android with our own views in Arc Search for Android's style: no Material, no imitation glass. Tokens and the type scale are in apps/mobile/AGENTS.md.
  • Two engines, one API. @bay/engine (modules/bay-engine): WebKit on iOS (1,867 lines of Swift), Bay's Chromium on Android (2,336 lines of Kotlin). Page scripts are shared JavaScript. Blocking is Bay's Rust blocker (packages/adblock) on both.
  • Shared first. Screens, panels, store and page scripts are shared; platform files are Name.ios.tsx / Name.android.tsx with one props type.
  • Testing. iOS completely; every UI change also on Android; finished flows become Argent e2e tests that run on both. Batch changes, run e2e once per batch through the MCP runner (apps/mobile/AGENTS.md, "Testing efficiently").
  • No server. Nothing leaves the phone except what a page or search engine asks for. AI, if any, runs on the device.
03 · Cra compared

The comparison is as of 15 November, before feat/mobile-cra. Items marked ✓ landed there; what the round left is under What is left.

Cra for iPhone, run next to Bay

Cra is an MIT-licensed Arc replacement (Swift, Chromium through CEF on the Mac). Its iPhone app sets out to match Arc Search 1.49 screen for screen: UIKit on WKWebView, 10,394 lines of Swift plus about 3,300 lines of layout and policy in CraCore/Mobile, measured from Arc Search's App Store footage and recordings, with 41 XCUITests. It is iPhone and iPad only, has a TestFlight beta, and its branch paused on 12 November (docs/ios-parity.md).

Both apps were built and run on the iPhone 18 Pro simulator (iOS 27) on 15 November: Cra from its main, Bay from feat/mobile-import (#24). Fresh installs, then search, a page, the page menu, site and app settings, the tab switcher and Spaces (Cra with its demo sidebar).

The short version. Cra is Arc Search, rebuilt carefully: fewer features, each finished, measured and explained to the user (onboarding, tips, per-site switches). Bay is a broader browser (Android, Reader, bookmarks and import, history, Peek, media, page tools, seven languages) with a plainer first run and a busier page menu. The useful things to take are mostly UX (open ready to type, show what blocking did, per-site blocker switches, swipe from anywhere, teach the gestures) and three platform features (on-device translation through Apple's framework, richer widgets and Siri shortcuts, alternate icons). On the UI side, Cra's measured sheets, page menu, switcher physics and springs port straight into Bay's shared code (UI to take); its Arc Search colours, copy and art stay out.

06 · Bay has, Cra lacks

Where Bay is ahead

  • Android. Cra has no Android app, and Arc Search for Android goes on 22 December. This is Bay's opening; nothing in the Cra list outranks R1.
  • Blocking depth. Brave's adblock-rust with uBlock Origin's lists, scriptlets and $redirect stand-ins, HTTPS upgrade, tracking-parameter stripping, AMP skipping and YouTube cleanup. Cra compiles a domain list into WebKit rules and hides cookie banners.
  • Reading and page tools. Reader with seven themes and three fonts (Cra's is a plain serif), Hide elements, Inspect with the font identifier, Copy as Markdown, Find with highlights.
  • Your data. Bookmarks, history, and import from Safari, Chrome, any bookmarks file and Arc Search (#24). Cra's phone has an archive only.
  • Links and media. Peek previews, image long-press, new windows as real tabs, the shared page-request sheet (dialogs, sign-in, camera, location), mini player, lock-screen controls, Picture in Picture.
  • Private tabs with Face ID, tabs that keep their history across relaunches, spaces made on the phone, seven interface languages, wallpapers and themes, crash reports.
07 · UI to take from Cra

Copy the structure, the numbers and the motion; keep Bay's brand

Cra is MIT-licensed, so its UI can be copied as long as its notice ships with Bay (R4). The code itself is UIKit and Bay is React Native, so the copy is a port, not a paste. The part worth taking is Packages/CraCore/Sources/CraCore/Mobile: about 3,300 lines of pure layout and motion rules (no UIKit), measured from Arc Search's footage, with 1,128 lines of tests. Those port to TypeScript and become shared code, so Android gets the same result as iOS.

Surface Cra's numbers and behaviour Bay today
Search sheet A full-width sheet that rises with the keyboard, 38 pt top corners, 48 pt field 16 pt in, 56 pt rows (two lines allowed), four rows and a fixed 302 pt height so it never jumps while typing. In landscape it keeps as many rows as fit. The page sits behind it as a dimmed card 10 pt in from each side. A tap above or a swipe down closes it. A floating card whose height follows the rows, over a dimmed page.
Page menu 38 pt sheet; a 47 pt address capsule; four 88.7 pt tiles filling the width (22 pt corners, labels under them); 48 pt rows with 14 pt corners; "Display Options" opens in place; a 55.7 pt footer card for what was blocked and the restore. Header, blocking switch, a nav row and twelve equal tiles.
Tab switcher Cards at 0.694 of the page with 17 pt corners. Cards behind dim up to 0.16 and shrink to 0.9, while the next card grows up to 1.02. Past either end the row follows at a third of the speed. A flick above 300 pt/s goes one card further. A card thrown up 120 pt (or 30 pt fast) closes. Dragging up from the bar shrinks the page under the finger, and 110 pt opens the switcher. Already close: same 0.56 step and a swipe-up close. No drag up from the bar, no dimming of the cards behind.
Motion Springs fitted to the footage are all critically damped (no overshoot): the row settles in 0.34 s and a card opens into its page in 0.26 s. One spring for everything (damping 22, stiffness 240, mass 0.9: damping ratio about 0.75, so it overshoots).
Settings sheets Arc Search 1.49's: a coloured rounded-square icon per row, 17 pt text in 52 pt rows, grouped cards, a back chevron and Done. Site Settings is its own 465 pt sheet over the page menu. Toggles show their state at a glance. Grouped rows with plain outline icons; site settings fold into the page menu's "This site".
First run, tips and cards Full-bleed animated gradient; large titles with one italic word; a phone-in-a-phone mock that runs the real interaction (pinch, a blocking switch on a demo page). A tip card over the bar with a ring round the button it points at. A dismissible promo card in the switcher. The dome welcome and an import card on the start page.
Share sheet "Saved" with a heart and check, a place picker that remembers the last choice, and Done; the link waits in an App Group inbox. Closes at once and opens Bay.

What not to copy

  • Arc Search's identity. Cra's colours are sampled from Arc Search (Palette.swift: the lavender-to-pink sheet, the #583dae → #c3699d gradient, #354FFF buttons). So are its copy ("scrubbed N trackers", "Get what you want twice as fast", the starter questions), its logo and fidget mark, and its widget gradient. Copying those would make Bay look like Arc Search, which The Browser Company still ships on iOS. Use Bay's own paper, dusk and accent colours, icon and wording.
  • The bar. Arc Search's bar (tab stack, "+" pill, ^) hides the address. Keep Bay's capsule with the host and the load's progress: it is where the search sheet grows from (A2) and it shows people where they are.
  • Pre-iOS 26 chrome. Cra opts out of Liquid Glass (UIDesignRequiresCompatibility). Keep glass on Bay's floating controls on iOS, but take Cra's flat, opaque sheets. Those are also how Bay's Android sheets look, so the two apps get closer.

10 · Decisions

Decisions in force

# Decision Why
D1 Android is Bay Chromium only; public test builds before 22 December. The system WebView fallback was dropped on 14 November. Arc Search's Android users are the opening.
D2 iOS blocking stays on WebKit content rules plus scriptlets. A web view can't intercept subresources on iOS. Measure it rather than chase it.
D3 Open: summarize on the device? Your call D6 ruled out Browse for Me, Summarize and Call Arc because they needed a paid server. Cra shows Summarize runs on the phone's own model. Recommended: allow Summarize after the first release (L3), keep Browse for Me and Call Arc out.
D4 Open: let people continue past a bad certificate? Your call Cra allows it per host for the run. Recommended: no; keep Bay's "This connection isn't private" page. The people who need it can use another browser, and it's an easy trap for everyone else.
D5 Copy Cra's UI structure, measurements and motion; keep Bay's brand. Cra is MIT, so ported rules keep its notice (R4). Its colours, copy, logo and widget art are Arc Search's, so Bay keeps its own (see "What not to copy"). Lens stays the reference for the start page, the bar and the glass.
D7 Sync and tablets after the first release. Unchanged.
D8 The Bergamot mirror needs your go-ahead before anything is published. Unchanged; C4 makes it Android-only.
11 · Risks

Risks

Risk Response
Bay Chromium's first compile fails late, and each runner round takes hours. Fix compile errors in batches per run; keep the saved tree fresh (it expires seven days after upload). R1 is the deadline that matters.
Play's closed-test rule delays the Android build past 22 December. Check the account type now; share the APK directly while the closed test runs.
Entitlements and Google's passkey list wait on others. Send R3 now; AutoFill and passkeys ship when granted, nothing else waits on them.
